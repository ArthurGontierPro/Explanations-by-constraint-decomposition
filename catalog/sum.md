## Constraint

There is no MiniZinc global called `sum`.

`grep -x -E 'sum' tools/data/minizinc-2.10.1-globals.txt` returns nothing; the file's 134 lines
contain `sliding_sum` (112), `sum_pred` (122) and `sum_set` (123) and no bare `sum`. So this
entry is named after an artifact, not after a global, and **it is the only one of the five this
session wrote for which `tools/mzn_coverage.py --rank` returns no row at all.**

The constraint the artifact is *about*, on the two in-repo statements of it, is:

`sum(array[1..n] of var int: x, var int: N)` — `N = Σ_i x[i]`.

**Provenance of the semantics — and it is weaker than every other entry's, which the repo
already flags.** `validator.ml:439-441` marks `Sum` as the exception in its ground-constraint
table: every other entry's semantics was read off the generator's decomposition table with line
numbers, and this one "is read off gccat's `sum_ctr` Purpose with CTR `=` and nothing else",

**But the producer is recoverable, and this entry recovers it.** `git log -- cata/sum.tex`
returns exactly one commit: **`3e4f17d`, 2020-08-10, "tests sum and regular"**. The same commit
carries a `sum` decomposition and its emitting call, at lines 361–363 and 388 of the generator
*as it stood then*:

```ocaml

           Decomp (4, rule1, [Global_devent (true, N, id, id, BC); Reified_devent (true, (B 3), id, id)]);
           Decomp (3, rule6, [Decomp_devent (true, (B 1), (function il -> sumi(sumt(forallp il))), p_out);

```

**It was deleted nine days later**, in `e973e1e`, 2020-08-19, whose commit message is
*"nouveaux systeme de propagation d'indice pour table Erreurs dans les autres contraintes
possibles"* — a new index-propagation system for `table`, with the author's own warning that
errors in the other constraints are possible. That commit rewrote **every other** `cata/*.tex`
and dropped both the `sum` value and its `explainall` line. `cata/sum.tex` was not rewritten,
because nothing produced it any more.

**So the file's real status is sharper than "orphaned".** `cata/sum.tex` is the one catalog
artifact that **predates the index-propagation rewrite** and has never been regenerated under
it — nor under the W1-T3 diagnostics, the W1-T7 first-order index ops, the W1-T9 defect fixes
or the W1-T2 refusal. Every other entry in `cata/` is output of today's generator; this one is
output of a 2020 generator that no longer exists, kept in the tree for six years.

**What the decomposition says, read off that recovered source.** It is the **order encoding**:

- **step 1, `rule1` (BC)** — `B1_{i,t} ⇔ X_i ≥ t`. Bounds consistency, so the channel is a
  threshold literal, not `X_i = t`.
- **step 2, `rule1` (BC)** — `B3_p ⇔ N ≥ p`, the same channel on the total.
- **step 3, `rule6`** — a Boolean sum in the `≥` direction: `B3_p ⇔ (Σ_{i,t} B1_{i,t} ≥ p)`.

Given `X_i ∈ [1,m]` and `t` ranging over `[1,m]`, `Σ_{t} [X_i ≥ t] = X_i`, so
`Σ_{i,t} B1_{i,t} = Σ_i X_i`; and `N ≥ p ⇔ Σ ≥ p` **for every** `p` is `N = Σ_i X_i`. The
one-directional `rule6` is therefore not a weakness — the equality comes from quantifying over
all thresholds `p`. **This is reasoning about the recovered source, not a run**; what
corroborates it is independent, and is the next paragraph.

**The validator's own encoding corroborates the order-encoding reading, by accident.**
`validator.ml:495` gives `Sum -> a.nv = sum_of xs` and `validator.ml:503` sets
`nv_range Sum = (sz.n, sz.n * sz.m)` — i.e. `N ∈ [n, n·m]`, exactly the range of `Σ_i X_i` for
`X_i ∈ [1,m]`. Those two lines were written from gccat's `sum_ctr`, with no access to the
deleted decomposition; they agree with it. That does not upgrade the provenance —
`validator.ml:439-441`'s warning still stands — but it is the first independent check the
orphan has ever had, and it holds.

**A note for `sum_pred`, because it changes that entry's pricing.** `decomps/sum_pred.md` says
the second line of a `sum` decomposition "has **no schema**", prices weighting by `t` at
**G11** and adding values rather than counting at **G12 + G13**, and concludes `E9` is unbuilt.
The orphan shows a route that needs none of those three: over a **BC** channel the order
encoding turns an integer sum into a *count of Booleans*, which `rule6` already does. What it
needs instead is a sum over **two index families at once** (`sumi ∘ sumt`). Whether that is
legal is not obvious — **G4** says `rule5/6/7` match `dee::[]` and `failwith "sommes multiples
pas encore implémentés"` otherwise (`docs/DECOMP_FORMAT_NOTES.md:43-49`) — and the recovered
source sidesteps it *syntactically*: there is one `Decomp_devent`, and both summations are
composed into its index modification. The two-family sum is visible in the shipped output (see
rule 1 below, whose single premise literal carries both an `∀i', i' ≠ i` and an `∀t', t' ≠ t`
sibling). **This paragraph is this session's reading of a deleted decomposition and one
artifact, not a measurement, and it contradicts a spec file** — see "How this entry was
produced".

**Spec:** none for `sum`. The nearest is `decomps/sum_pred.md` (shape **S12**), which is

## Generated rules

Rendered from `cata/sum.tex` (single line, no trailing newline;
`grep -o '\\frac' cata/sum.tex | wc -l` → **4**). `[1,n]` is the variable index set, `[1,m]`
the value set, `p` the threshold on the total.

### Rule 1 — `X_i ≥ t`

```
X_{i'} < t' ,  ∀i' ,  i' ∈ [1,n] ,  i' ≠ i ,  ∀t' ,  t' ∈ [1,m] ,  t' ≠ t
N ≥ p ,  ∀p ,  p ∈ [1,n]
------------------------------------------------------------------------- ⊢
X_{i} ≥ t
```

**Verdict:** `SOUND but NOT MINIMAL — droppable: #2 (N >= p)`
**Reading(s) checked:** 2 (2 sound, 0 unsound) — `forall i',forall t' ; forall p` and
`forall t',forall i' ; forall p`. Store sweep agrees with the singleton reduction.

Note the **two** sum siblings on one premise literal: `i'` and `t'` both carry the
`∀ … ∈ … , ≠` pattern that `sum_node` builds (`explenation generator.ml:96`). That is the
two-family sum discussed above, visible in the output.

### Rule 2 — `X_i < t`

```
X_{i'} ≥ t' ,  ∀i' ,  i' ∈ [1,n] ,  i' ≠ i ,  ∀t' ,  t' ∈ [1,m] ,  t' ≠ t
N < p ,  ∀p ,  p ∈ [1,n]
------------------------------------------------------------------------- ⊢
X_{i} < t
```

**Verdict:** `VACUOUS — no store in scope satisfies the premises, so it is sound only because
it can never fire`
**Reading(s) checked:** 2 (2 sound, 0 unsound), both marked `(premises never hold)`. Store
sweep agrees.

### Rule 3 — `N ≥ p`

```
X_{i} ≥ t ,  ∀t ,  t ∈ [1,m] ,  ∀i ,  i ∈ [1,n]

```

**Verdict:** `SOUND but NOT MINIMAL — droppable: #1 (X_i >= t)`
**Reading(s) checked:** 2 (2 sound, 0 unsound) — `forall t,forall i` and `forall i,forall t`.

### Rule 4 — `N < p`

## Status

**`flagged`** — every generated rule is flagged, which is the legend's definition. Two are
`SOUND but NOT MINIMAL` and two are `VACUOUS`; none is `UNSOUND`, and none is `SOUND and
MINIMAL`.

**All four verdicts have one cause, and it is a known and already-fixed defect that could not
reach this file.** `p` ranges over `[1,n]` and is printed as a **universally bound** index,
`∀p, p ∈ [1,n]`. So:

- the premise `∀p ∈ [1,n]: N ≥ p` means `N ≥ n`, which is *always* true in scope
  (`N = Σ_i X_i ≥ n` for `X_i ≥ 1`) — hence rules 1 and 3 have a droppable premise, and hence
  rule 3's conclusion holds with no premise at all;
- the premise `∀p ∈ [1,n]: N < p` means `N < 1`, which is *never* true — hence rules 2 and 4
  are `VACUOUS`.

That is the same defect W1-T9 diagnosed and fixed for `gcc`: "`∀p ∈ [1,n]: O_t ≥ p` … collapses
to `O_t ≥ n`", where `p` "is quantified over at the CONSTRAINT level, so an explanation built
from it is a schema valid for each `p` separately"; the fix was `OpPoint`/`pointp`, a ranged
but **unbound** index, and it took all four `gcc` rules from flagged to sound and minimal
(`explenation generator.ml:815-826`). **`sum.tex` has no producer, so the fix could not be
applied to it, and it is the last place in the tree where the pre-W1-T9 rendering survives.**

**This is a prediction, and it is falsifiable in one commit.** Port the recovered
decomposition, replace the `forallp` in step 3's descending modification with `pointp`,
regenerate, re-run `make validate`. The reasoning above says rules 2 and 4 stop being `VACUOUS`
and rules 1 and 3 stop being `NOT MINIMAL`. **Nobody has run that**, and this entry does not
claim the outcome; it claims that the four current verdicts are explained by the binder and
that W1-T6 is therefore worth doing rather than resolving by deletion.

**Two caveats on the verdicts themselves, both from `docs/VALIDATOR.md`.** The sizes are
`n, m ∈ {2,3,4}` with the store sweep held at `n = m = 2` (`docs/VALIDATOR.md:185`), and the
ground semantics is hand-encoded from gccat rather than derived from the generator's `ind_op`
data — for this entry uniquely, from gccat *alone*.

## Calibration (W3-T5, D-0013)

**Verdict: no published rule exists.**

`CHRISTMAS_LIST.md` carries no `sum` row, and the nearest one, `sum_pred` at line 213, has
`none` in the literature column. There is nothing sourced in `catalog/_literature/` and nothing
to source.

**The honest reading of "no published rule" here is different from `among`'s, and the entry
should not hide that.** For `among`, `none` is an assertion about a global constraint the index
surveyed. For `sum`, the index simply has no row, because a linear sum is not a MiniZinc global
— it is a primitive that every LCG solver explains natively, and explaining a linear constraint
is standard and old. So `no published rule exists` is accurate *about this repo's index* and
should not be read as a claim that nobody has published an explanation of a linear sum. **A
future session that wants to calibrate this entry should expect to find literature, and should
put it under `sum_pred` (or `sliding_sum`, `CHRISTMAS_LIST.md:151`) rather than here.**

Nothing is compared on minimality: `catalog/README.md` records that no paper sourced so far
proves its explanation minimal, and this entry's two `NOT MINIMAL` verdicts are a validator
result about premise-droppability, not a comparison with anyone.
